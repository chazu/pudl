pudl guide policies — per-resource compliance rules

A policy says what a good instance of one type looks like. It is never
assigned to records. Every import and observation evaluates each applicable
policy per resource: compliant, noncompliant, or unknown (a constrained
field was absent). Rules that span resources are Datalog checks instead.

WRITE ONE

    package gcp

    #FirewallLogging: {
        _pudl: {schema_type: "policy", base_schema: "gcp.#Firewall"}
        logConfig: {
            enable: true @pudl(rule="firewall-logging", severity="low", doc="Log rule matches")
        } @pudl(absent="violation")
        sourceRanges?: [...(!="0.0.0.0/0")] @pudl(rule="world-ingress", severity="high")
    }

  A policy is a standalone definition linked by base_schema. Do not write
  #Policy: #Type & {...}; it does not load. A policy whose base_schema names
  another policy extends it.

  @pudl keys: rule, severity (low|medium|high|critical), doc, and
  absent="violation" (a missing field is a violation, not unknown; also
  implied for required fields of a type with review status validated).

GOTCHAS

  - && and || do not short-circuit over absent fields; the policy silently
    passes. Test present values with list.Contains([if x != _|_ {x}], v).
  - Annotate fields outside comprehensions (f?: _ @pudl(rule=...)), then
    constrain them inside; otherwise the policy fails to load.
  - A field required only inside a comprehension is never reported absent.
    Put the rule on a field that is present (an anchor) as a conflict.
  - A policy cannot constrain a sensitive field; its value is redacted.

TRY, INSTALL, READ

  pudl policy evaluate --what-if draft.cue      evaluate a draft; writes nothing
  pudl schema add gcp.firewall-logging draft.cue
  pudl policy evaluate --dry-run                what a refresh would change
  pudl policy evaluate                          refresh stored results
  pudl policy list --type gcp.#Firewall         counts, review status, stale
  pudl policy show gcp.#FirewallBestPractice --owner platform
  pudl show <entry-id>                          compliance section and record

IN CHECKS

  Results are facts: policy_status and policy_violation. The built-in rule
  policy_noncompliant(resource, policy, severity) feeds checks:

    {name: "policy-high", query: "policy_noncompliant", expect: "empty",
     severity: "fail", message: "Fix policy violations", evidence: ["model:firewalls"]}

  pudl query policy_noncompliant severity=high

  Details and worked patterns: docs/schema-authoring.md#compliance-policies,
  packs/gcp/schema/gcp/policies.cue.
